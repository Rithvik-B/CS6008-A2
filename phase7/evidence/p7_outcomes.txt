### Phase 7.3 -- SIX outcomes (3 attacks x 2 clients)
### Client R = STRICT (refuses cleartext, validates cert). Client P = PERMISSIVE (falls back to cleartext).

attack                           | R: refuses cleartext         | P: permits cleartext        
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
(i)  alter answer in transit     | REAL .20 (cannot alter)      | REAL .20 (DoT works)        
(ii) rogue certificate           | FAILS CLOSED (cert rejected) | POISONED .30 (downgrade)    
(iii) block 853 (deny DoT)       | FAILS CLOSED (timeout)       | POISONED .30 (downgrade)    

The strict client is never poisoned (ii/iii are only denial-of-service). The permissive
client is poisoned whenever an attack breaks DoT, because it then downgrades to cleartext
where the Phase-6 forgery (or the rogue resolver) lands. Attack (i) breaks nothing, so no
downgrade is triggered and both clients keep the genuine, encrypted answer.
