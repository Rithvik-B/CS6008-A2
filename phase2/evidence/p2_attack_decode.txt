### Phase 2 attack capture (attacker eth0): interception + injection of example.com
### The attacker is the sole deliverer of replies to the resolver (core-router diverts
### them here). For each reply it rewrites the Ethernet dst to the resolver and injects;
### only the example.com A record is changed, everything else is relayed byte-for-byte.
###
### MACs:  ea:93:66:26:29:d8 = core-router (genuine reply arriving at attacker)
###        02:96:fd:d8:c7:79 = attacker   (frame injected toward the resolver)
###        02:ec:87:d5:68:8e = resolver   (injection destination)
###
### frame  eth.src      ip.src        dns.a (answer)            note
### -----------------------------------------------------------------------------------
###   7    core-router  172.16.3.30   172.16.1.20,172.16.3.30   GENUINE domain answer IN
###   8    attacker     172.16.3.30   172.16.1.30,172.16.3.30   FORGED  answer OUT -> resolver
###
### i.e. the genuine A=172.16.1.20 (frame 7) is rewritten to A=172.16.1.30 (frame 8) and
### delivered to the resolver. The root/com referrals (frames 1-6) are relayed unchanged.
###
### NOTE on checksums: captured frames on this Docker veth path carry offloaded/partial
### UDP checksums, which tshark cannot verify (it flags them). The interceptor recomputes
### a full IP+UDP checksum on every injected frame; the proof it is valid is that the
### validating-OFF resolver ACCEPTS the forged answer, caches it, and serves 172.16.1.30
### to the client (Linux silently drops UDP datagrams with a wrong checksum).
